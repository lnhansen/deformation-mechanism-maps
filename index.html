About these maps

Each mechanism gives a strain rate at every point of the map. Mechanisms add in parallel unless they share a series group, in which case the slower one limits the group. The coloured field is the mechanism contributing the most strain rate; thin lines are contours of the total strain rate (or viscosity, σ/3ε̇).

Power law: ε̇ = A σn d−p COHr eαφ exp[−(E + PV)/RT], with an optional near-solidus enhancement exp[−ΔQ/R (1/T − 1/Tc)] above Tc = f Ts (Hiraga).

Peierls law: ε̇ = A σn exp{−(E + PV)/RT [1 − (σ/σP)p]q}.

Breithaupt model (Breithaupt, Katz, Hansen & Kumamoto, PNAS 2023): steady state of the Taylor-stress evolution equation with dynamic recovery and static recovery by pipe, grain-boundary and lattice diffusion, ε̇ = A′σρ² sinh[(σ − σρ − σd)/σref]. Its field is split by what limits the rate: low-temperature plasticity (dynamic recovery), grain-size-insensitive creep (pipe-diffusion recovery), or grain-size-sensitive creep (grain-boundary recovery).

Units: stress MPa, grain size µm, temperature K, pressure GPa, activation energy kJ/mol, activation volume cm³/mol, strain rate s⁻¹. Pre-exponents are in those units.

Rock and Mineral Physics Laboratory, University of Minnesota. Flow laws are taken from the cited papers; check parameters against the source before use in publications.
