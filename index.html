About these maps

Each mechanism gives a strain rate at every point of the map. Mechanisms add in parallel unless they share a series group, in which case the slower one limits the group. The coloured field is the mechanism contributing the most strain rate; thin lines are contours of the total strain rate (or viscosity, σ/3ε̇).

Power law: ε̇ = A σn d−p COHr eαφ exp[−(E + PV)/RT], with an optional near-solidus enhancement exp[−ΔQ/R (1/T − 1/Tc)] above Tc = f Ts (Yabe & Hiraga 2020). The Treatise maps use Ts = 1350 °C.

Peierls law: ε̇ = A σn exp{−(E + PV)/RT [1 − (σ/σP)p]q}.

Breithaupt model (Breithaupt, Katz, Hansen & Kumamoto, PNAS 2023): steady state of the Taylor-stress evolution equation with dynamic recovery and static recovery by pipe, grain-boundary and lattice diffusion, ε̇ = A′σρ² sinh[(σ − σρ − σd)/σref]. Its field is split by the recovery process that balances storage: low-temperature plasticity (dynamic recovery), grain-size-insensitive (GSI) dislocation creep (recovery by pipe diffusion), or grain-size-sensitive (GSS) dislocation creep (recovery by grain-boundary diffusion).

Piezometers (stress–grain-size maps): Karato et al. (1980), d = 8.3×10³ σ−1.18, dashed; Meyers & Kohlstedt (2026), d = 315 σ−0.71, dash-dot (d in µm, σ in MPa).

Units: stress MPa, grain size µm, temperature °C (10⁴/T in K⁻¹; flow laws are evaluated in K), pressure GPa, activation energy kJ/mol, activation volume cm³/mol, strain rate s⁻¹. Pre-exponents are in those units.

Rock and Mineral Physics Laboratory, University of Minnesota. Flow laws are taken from the cited papers; check parameters against the source before use in publications.
